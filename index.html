South Asia's best-kept secret

Rivers, mangroves, tea hills and the world's longest beach

Bangladesh is a land of green deltas, ancient ruins and warm hospitality. Here is everything you need to plan your first visit.

Explore destinations Plan your trip

Why visit Bangladesh

Wild nature

Sail into the Sundarbans, the largest mangrove forest in the world and home to the Royal Bengal tiger.

Deep history

Walk through Buddhist monasteries, Mughal forts and a 15th-century mosque city, some of them on the UNESCO list.

Rich food

From smoky Old Dhaka biryani to fresh hilsa curry and winter pitha, every region has its own flavors.

Warm people

Visitors are still rare, so expect curious smiles, cups of tea and plenty of invitations.

Top destinations

Filter by the kind of trip you're after.

Beach

Cox's Bazar

An unbroken stretch of sand along the Bay of Bengal, often called the world's longest natural sea beach. Take a day trip along the Marine Drive to Inani and Himchari.

Beach

Saint Martin's Island

The country's only coral island, with coconut palms and clear water. Visitor numbers and seasons are regulated to protect the reef, so check the current rules before you go.

Nature · UNESCO

The Sundarbans

The world's largest mangrove forest and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Multi-day boat trips from Khulna or Mongla pass through creeks where you may spot deer, crocodiles and kingfishers.

Nature

Sylhet & Ratargul

Paddle a wooden boat through the flooded Ratargul swamp forest, then head to Jaflong, where clear rivers flow down from the Khasi hills.

Nature · Hills

Srimangal

The tea capital of Bangladesh. Walk through rolling estates, look for gibbons in Lawachara National Park, and try the famous layered "seven-color" tea.

Hills

Bandarban

Misty ridges, waterfalls and indigenous villages in the Chittagong Hill Tracts. Watch the sunrise above the clouds at Nilgiri. Some areas need permits.

Hills · Nature

Rangamati

Take a boat across the turquoise arms of Kaptai Lake, cross the hanging bridge and browse handwoven textiles from the local communities.

City · Heritage

Old Dhaka

Ride a rickshaw through lanes full of noise and color to the pink Ahsan Manzil palace, Lalbagh Fort and the busy Sadarghat river port.

Heritage · UNESCO

Paharpur

The ruins of Somapura Mahavihara, a vast 8th-century Buddhist monastery that was once one of the great centers of learning in South Asia.

Heritage · UNESCO

Bagerhat Mosque City

A 15th-century city founded by Khan Jahan Ali. Its centerpiece is the brick Shat Gombuj (Sixty Dome) Mosque. It pairs well with a Sundarbans trip.

Heritage

Mahasthangarh

Near Bogura, the remains of ancient Pundranagara, one of the oldest urban archaeological sites in Bangladesh.

Nature

Jaflong

Stony riverbeds and clear water right at the foot of the hills on the Indian border. Easy to visit as a day trip from Sylhet city.

Travel tips

Visa

Many nationalities can get a visa on arrival, but it depends on your passport and the rules change. Check the official portal visa.gov.bd or your nearest Bangladesh embassy before you travel.

Money

The currency is the Bangladeshi taka (BDT). ATMs are common in cities but rare in rural areas, so carry cash when you head out of town. Mobile wallets like bKash are widely used.

Getting around

Domestic flights link Dhaka with Sylhet, Chattogram, Cox's Bazar and Jashore. Trains are comfortable and scenic. River launches are a classic experience, and rickshaws are best for short trips.

Dress & etiquette

Bangladesh is a conservative, mostly Muslim country. Cover your shoulders and knees, take off your shoes at mosques and homes, and use your right hand to eat and to pass things.

Stay connected

Local SIM cards are cheap and easy to buy with your passport. Grameenphone and Robi have wide coverage.

Safety

Check your government's travel advisory before you go, especially for the Chittagong Hill Tracts, where permits may be required. Traffic is intense, so take care when crossing roads.

Food to try

  • Kacchi biryani

    Slow-cooked rice and mutton, an Old Dhaka classic.

  • Ilish (hilsa)

    The national fish, often cooked in mustard sauce.

  • Fuchka

    Crisp shells filled with spiced tamarind water. The favorite street snack.

  • Pitha

    Rice cakes, both sweet and savory, especially in winter.

  • Bhorta

    Mashed vegetables, fish or lentils with mustard oil and chili.

  • Mishti doi

    Sweet, caramelized yogurt served in clay pots.
